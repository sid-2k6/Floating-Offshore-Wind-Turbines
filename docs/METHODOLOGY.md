## 1. Starting point

### FLOATBench

FLOATBench (arXiv:2605.25717, CC-BY-4.0) provides 582,120 fatigue-damage labels:
three 22 MW floating tower geometries (`ref`, `opt1`, `opt2`) on a common
three-column semi-submersible, each with 6,468 ten-minute OpenFAST simulations
(1,078 wind/wave operating points × 6 turbulence seeds) labelled at 30 tower
cross-sections.

Per row: `mean_wind_speed`, `std_wind_speed`, `wave_hs`, `wave_tp`, section
geometry, `damage` (Miner-summed), `damage_weight` (25-year occurrence
probability).

Critically, all runs follow IEC 61400-3-2 DLC 1.2 with the **same** baseline
controller and `IPC_ControlMode = 0`. There is no control variation, no time
axis, and no sensor model.

### What must be added

| missing | supplied by |
|---|---|
| rotor response to pitch/yaw | official IEA-22-280-RWT performance surfaces (§2) |
| controllable vs uncontrollable damage split | NNLS fit on FLOATBench (§3) |
| damage under action | ratio anchoring (§4) |
| actuator limits and duty | official ROSCO controller file (§5) |
| episodes / time axis | random walk on the operating grid (§6) |
| reward | lifetime-weighted fatigue vs energy vs duty (§7) |
| sensor realism | IoT error model (§8) |

## 2. Rotor aerodynamics: measured, not fitted

Two official artefacts from `IEAWindSystems/IEA-22-280-RWT` (Apache-2.0):

**Baseline operating schedule** — 22 OpenFAST steady-state points giving
`wind_speed, rotor_speed, pitch, tip_speed_ratio, rotor_thrust,
mechanical_power, electrical_power`. From these:

```
Ct_base(V) = T / (½ ρ A V²)          Cp_base(V) = P_mech / (½ ρ A V³)
```

with ρ = 1.225 kg/m³, A = 63,347 m² (D = 284 m). Range: `Ct` 0.840 → 0.053,
`Cp_max` = 0.496.

**Rotor performance surfaces** — a 20 × 20 grid of `Cp(θ, λ)`, `Ct(θ, λ)`,
`Cq(θ, λ)` over pitch −5…45° and TSR 2…14, generated by the ROSCO toolbox from
the actual blade geometry and airfoil polars. Bilinearly interpolated, clamped
at the grid edges.

### Ratio anchoring

The two artefacts come from different solver configurations and disagree by
~28 % in mean relative terms on `Ct` (Pearson r = 0.994), mostly where `Ct` is
small. Rather than reconcile them, the surface is used **only** for the
dimensionless relative effect of an action:

```
ct_ratio(V, Δθ) = Ct_surface(θ_base(V) + Δθ, λ(V)) / Ct_surface(θ_base(V), λ(V))
```

Any systematic multiplicative bias in the surface cancels exactly. Absolute
thrust and power then come from the steady-state points:

```
T(V, Δθ, γ) = T_base(V) · ct_ratio(V, Δθ) · cos^2.00(γ)
P(V, Δθ, γ) = min( P_base(V) · cp_ratio(V, Δθ) · cos^1.88(γ),  P_rated )
```

The yaw thrust exponent 2.0 is the momentum-consistent normal-component scaling;
the power exponent 1.88 is a widely used empirical value.

### Monotonicity guard

Below rated, the reference pitch schedule and the performance surface do not put
the `Cp` optimum at the same pitch angle (different solvers; the reference
schedule includes peak shaving). Unguarded, a positive pitch offset could
*increase* power. Both ratios are therefore capped at 1 for `Δθ ≥ 0`: feathering
must never gain power or thrust. Negative offsets are excluded from the action
space (§5).

### Sanity check

`dP/dθ` at +1° above rated: −2.34 MW/deg at 14 m/s, −3.34 at 18, −4.21 at 22.
The right order for a 22 MW rotor. Enforced by
`test_pitch_sensitivity_is_physically_plausible`.

### Thrust sensitivity to wind

With `T = ½ ρ A Ct V²` and `Ct` locally insensitive to `V` at fixed `(λ, θ)`:

```
dT/dV = ρ A Ct V
```

This converts turbulent wind fluctuations into thrust fluctuations, and is the
bridge into the fatigue model.

## 3. Load-path decomposition: fitted on FLOATBench

### Why a decomposition is needed

Rotor control cannot touch wave-driven tower fatigue. Without separating the
paths, the model would let an agent pitch away damage that is physically
hydrodynamic. The split must therefore be *estimated*, and it is estimated from
FLOATBench rather than assumed.

### Model

For a single-slope S-N curve with Wöhler exponent `m`, damage scales as load
amplitude to the power `m`, and independent load processes add:

```
D(condition, s) ≈ Σ_k  c_{s,k} · A_k(condition)^m ,   m = 3
```

FLOATBench states `DEL ∝ D^(1/m)` with `m = 3`, consistent with the single-slope
welded-steel curve of DNV-RP-C203.

Four basis amplitudes:

| k | `A_k` | rationale |
|---|---|---|
| 0 `thrust_turbulence` | `ρ A Ct(V) V σ_V` | thrust std, using FLOATBench's own realised `std_wind_speed` |
| 1 `rotor_cyclic` | `½ ρ A Ct(V) V²` | mean thrust, as the scale for nP cyclic loading |
| 2 `wave_quasistatic` | `Hs · DAF(Tp)` | wave elevation, amplified near tower FA1 |
| 3 `wave_inertial` | `Hs / Tp²` | platform-acceleration-driven inertial loading |

with a single-DOF dynamic amplification factor

```
DAF = 1 / sqrt( (1 − r²)² + (2 ζ r)² ) ,      r = (1/Tp) / f_FA1
```

and per-tower `f_FA1` from FLOATBench Table 4 (0.336 / 0.573 / 0.537 Hz).
Because the wave band (1/16.5…1/6 Hz) sits well below all three tower
frequencies, `r < 0.5` throughout and the DAF is a mild, monotone amplification
(1.0–1.33) rather than a resonance spike.

### Fit

Columns are normalised by their means, raised to the power `m`, and fitted
per-section by **non-negative** least squares against that section's 6,468 real
damage values. Non-negativity is what makes the coefficients interpretable as
load-path contributions.

Damage spans about six orders of magnitude, so rows are weighted by
`damage^(−p)`; `p` and `ζ` are grid searched jointly, selecting on mean R² in
DEL space (FLOATBench's own regression target). `p = 1/3` and `ζ = 0.005` win for
all three towers.

| tower | R²(DEL) mean / min / max | R²(D) mean | DEL median rel. err |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ref`  | 0.732 / 0.720 / 0.775 | 0.553 | 18.0 % |
| `opt1` | 0.793 / 0.761 / 0.799 | 0.694 | 12.5 % |
| `opt2` | 0.802 / 0.760 / 0.811 | 0.716 | 12.2 % |

Mean path shares (`opt2`): turbulence 0.041, cyclic 0.267, wave quasi-static
0.520, wave inertial 0.173. Wave loading dominates on average, which is expected
for a floating platform over an envelope reaching `Hs` = 8.8 m.

### Controllable share

```
φ(condition, s) = (share_turbulence + share_cyclic)
```

This is the ceiling on achievable relief. Mean 0.21–0.32, p05 ≈ 0.01,
p95 ≈ 0.73–0.88 — control authority varies enormously across the envelope, and
that variation is the main source of state-dependence in the RL problem.

### Known collinearity

`A_0` and `A_1` correlate at r = 0.888 (they share the `Ct` factor and differ by
`2σ_V/V`; turbulence intensity spans 0.11–0.35, which breaks the degeneracy).
The wave columns correlate at r = 0.648. Collinearity within the *wave* group is
harmless because both wave gains are fixed at 1, so their internal split never
affects the action response. Only the aero-vs-wave boundary and the
turbulence-vs-cyclic split matter, and both are identified.

## 4. Damage under action: ratio anchoring

Per-load-path gains:

```
g_turb    = ct_ratio
g_cyc     = ct_ratio · (1 + yaw_cyclic_gain·|sin γ|) · (1 − ipc_authority·κ)
g_wave_qs = 1
g_wave_in = 1
```

`ct_ratio` already contains the yaw `cos²` thrust reduction. The extra cyclic
factor captures the opposite effect: yawing lowers mean thrust but raises
once-per-revolution load variation, because each blade sweeps a non-axisymmetric
inflow. IPC attacks the cyclic term only.

Damage follows in closed form:

```
D_out(s) = D_floatbench(s) · [ Σ_k c_{s,k}(g_k A_k)^m ] / [ Σ_k c_{s,k} A_k^m ]
```

Equivalently, for a single controllable path, `D_out/D_in = 1 − φ + φ·g^m` —
the relief is bounded by the controllable share.

Verified action effects at `opt2`, averaged over all 6,468 conditions:

| action | damage ratio | power | thrust |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 1.0000 | 16.97 MW | 1.640 MN |
| pitch +2° | 0.9077 | 12.77 MW | 1.288 MN |
| pitch +4° | 0.8416 | 8.14 MW | 0.922 MN |
| pitch +8° | 0.7737 | 2.59 MW | 0.369 MN |
| yaw error 15° | 1.2641 | 15.90 MW | 1.530 MN |
| yaw error 30° | 1.2704 | 12.95 MW | 1.230 MN |
| IPC full | 0.8585 | 16.97 MW | 1.640 MN |
| pitch +4° + IPC | 0.7906 | 8.14 MW | 0.922 MN |

Note yaw misalignment *increases* tower damage — the cyclic penalty outweighs
the mean-thrust reduction — while IPC gives relief at no power cost.

### Section reduction

Fatigue life is set by the governing cross-section, so the reward uses
`damage_max` over the 30 sections. `damage_base`, `damage_top`, `damage_mean` and
`damage_sum` are also emitted, since FLOATBench shows base and top behave
differently.

## 5. Action space and actuators

| action | range | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `pitch_offset_deg` | [0, 8] | collective offset added to the baseline schedule, towards feather |
| `yaw_setpoint_deg` | [−30, 30] | commanded yaw relative to measured inflow; 0 = track the wind |
| `ipc_level` | [0, 1] | IPC activation |

Only feathering offsets are allowed: negative offsets move towards stall, and the
official limit `PC_MinPit = −0.07 rad = −4.0°` leaves little margin anyway.

Actuator limits from `IEA-22-280-RWT-Semi_DISCON.IN`:

```
PC_MaxRat       = 0.03491 rad/s → 2.00  °/s   pitch rate
Y_Rate          = 0.00870 rad/s → 0.499 °/s   yaw rate
Y_ErrThresh     = 4 / 8 °                      yaw deadband
IPC_ControlMode = 0                            IPC off in the baseline
```

A 600 s step is long compared with actuator dynamics, so these do not bind
within a step. They are used instead to convert commanded changes into duty:

```
duty_total = (pitch_travel + 0.1·pitch_hold + yaw_engagement + ipc_level) / 3.1
```

where `yaw_engagement` is the fraction of the step spent slewing at `Y_Rate`
(zero if the command is inside the deadband). Per-step setpoint change caps
(4°, 15°, 0.5) keep commanded trajectories smooth; these are operational choices,
not hard limits.

## 6. MDP construction

### Episodes

Each FLOATBench condition sits at a grid coordinate
`(wind_speed_id, wave_hs_id, wave_tp_id)` on a 22 × 7 × 7 envelope with 6 seeds.
An episode is a correlated random walk: each coordinate holds with probability
0.55 or steps ±1, clipped at the bounds. Because FLOATBench sampled wind and
waves from a *joint* distribution, walking the grid keeps the wind–wave pairing
physically consistent. A fresh turbulence seed is drawn each step.

36 steps × 600 s = 6 hours per episode. 1,200 episodes × 3 towers = 3,600
episodes, 129,600 transitions, 21,600 simulated hours.

### Inflow direction

FLOATBench simulates aligned wind and waves and carries no direction, so
direction is introduced as an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process (σ = 12°, 2-hour
correlation, clipped ±45°) — this is what gives the yaw action something to do.
A per-episode static vane bias (σ = 2.5°) is added, since standing yaw
misalignment from vane miscalibration is common on operating turbines.

### Plant

The nacelle is a rate- and deadband-limited tracker of the commanded heading:

```
target  = measured_direction − yaw_setpoint
Δ       = target − nacelle_yaw
travel  = sign(Δ)·min(|Δ|, Y_Rate·Δt)   if |Δ| > Y_ErrThresh   else 0
γ       = inflow_direction − nacelle_yaw
```

Residual `γ` feeds both the aerodynamic model and the cyclic load term.

### Behaviour policies

The dataset is populated by a deliberate mixture, so an offline algorithm sees
both the uncontrolled reference and a known-good low-cost strategy alongside
uniform exploration: `baseline` 0.20, `random` 0.40, `ipc_only` 0.15,
`feather` 0.15, `yaw_seeker` 0.10.

## 7. Reward

```
reward = w_f · fatigue_relief · severity − w_p · power_loss_fraction − w_d · duty_total
       = 2.0 · (1 − DEL_ctrl/DEL_ref) · severity − 1.0 · ΔP/P_rated − 0.05 · duty
```

All terms dimensionless and bounded, so the weights are directly comparable.

### Severity weighting

Damage varies by ~6 orders of magnitude. Unweighted, a 10 % DEL reduction in a
benign sea state would score the same as one in the storm conditions that
actually consume tower life, and the optimum would degenerate to a constant
action. `severity` is built from FLOATBench's own `damage_weight`:

```
severity = clip( (D_base·damage_weight)^(1/m) / p90_reference , 0, 2 )
```

so relief is rewarded in proportion to the lifetime damage it avoids.

### The reference point

The reference is the *aerodynamically ideal* baseline: zero pitch offset, zero
IPC, perfect yaw alignment. An uncontrolled real turbine scores slightly below
zero against it, because vane bias and the 8° deadband leave a standing
misalignment — so correcting that misalignment is itself rewarded, which mirrors
a genuine value-add of active yaw control.

### Resulting structure

Mean reward by wind band and fixed policy (`opt2`):

| wind | baseline | feather | IPC only | controllable share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–6 | −0.006 | **+0.007** | −0.004 | 0.229 |
| 6–9 | −0.060 | **+0.128** | +0.073 | 0.650 |
| 9–12 | −0.105 | **+0.188** | +0.151 | 0.788 |
| 12–16 | −0.036 | −0.273 | **+0.040** | 0.452 |
| 16–20 | **−0.007** | −0.486 | −0.015 | 0.156 |
| 20+ | **−0.006** | −0.609 | −0.017 | 0.044 |

Best fixed policy over 40 episodes in the online env: `ipc_full` +1.80,
`baseline` −1.45, `feather2+ipc` −4.72, `yaw20` −8.04, `feather4` −13.55.
A learned policy should beat IPC-only by feathering *selectively*.

## 8. IoT sensor layer

Eight channels, each with calibration bias, random-walk drift, absolute +
proportional noise, quantisation, latency and packet loss. Errors are drawn
per-episode, so they are correlated within an episode and independent across
episodes — how real instrumentation behaves between maintenance visits.

| channel | instrument | noise | dropout | latency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `wind_speed` | nacelle anemometer | 0.4 m/s + 3 % | 0.5 % | 0 |
| `turbulence_std` | derived from anemometer | 0.15 + 8 % | 1 % | 1 step |
| `wind_direction` | wind vane | 3° | 0.5 % | 0 |
| `wave_hs` | wave radar / buoy | 0.15 m + 5 % | 2 % | 1 step |
| `wave_tp` | wave radar spectrum | 0.5 s + 3 % | 2 % | 1 step |
| `tower_damage_rate` | strain gauge rosette | 6 % | 1 % | 0 |
| `thrust` | strain + rotor state estimate | 4 % | 0.5 % | 0 |
| `power` | SCADA transducer | 0.5 % | 0.2 % | 0 |

Outputs `meas_*`, `valid_*` and `sensor_health`. Realised errors are reported in
the manifest (e.g. 12.9 % median relative error on turbulence intensity, 8.7 % on
`Hs`, 3.9 % on damage rate). `--iot-severity` scales all noise; `--no-iot`
reproduces perfect sensing for ablation.

## 10. Parameter provenance

Every number in the pipeline falls into one of four categories.

### Measured (official reference data)

Rotor diameter, hub height, rated power, cut-in/out, rated rotor speed, optimal
TSR, air density; the 22 steady-state operating points; the 400-point Cp/Ct/Cq
surface; pitch rate, yaw rate, yaw deadband, pitch limits; all FLOATBench damage
values, environmental conditions and occurrence weights; the three tower FA1
frequencies.

### Fitted from FLOATBench

The 4 × 30 per-section load-path coefficients per tower (360 in total), the
damping ratio ζ, and the residual weighting exponent `p`.

### Standard engineering relations

`Ct = T/(½ρAV²)`; `dT/dV = ρACtV`; single-DOF DAF; `m = 3` Wöhler exponent
(FLOATBench's stated value, per DNV-RP-C203); yaw thrust exponent 2.0
(momentum-consistent).

### Literature-informed — the honest weak points

| parameter | value | note |
|---|---|---|
| `yaw_cyclic_gain` | 1.5 | 1.75× cyclic amplitude at 30° misalignment |
| `ipc_authority` | 0.30 | cyclic amplitude cancelled at full IPC |
| yaw power exponent | 1.88 | common empirical value |
| IoT channel parameters | see §8 | representative, not from datasheets |
| OU direction process | σ 12°, τ 2 h | plausible, not site-specific |
| vane bias | σ 2.5° | plausible magnitude |

The first two directly scale control authority for yaw and IPC. Both are
configurable via `ControlAuthorityConfig`; treat conclusions that hinge on them
as conditional and run a sensitivity study. See
[`LIMITATIONS.md`](LIMITATIONS.md).
